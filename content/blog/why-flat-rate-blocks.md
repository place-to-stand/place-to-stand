The first question every prospective client asks is "how much?" The honest
answer for bespoke software is "it depends" — but that is not an answer, it
is a hedge. So we needed a pricing model that gave a real answer without
forcing us to invent fictional certainty up front.

## The two models we rejected

**Hourly billing.** Hourly punishes the buyer for our learning curve and
punishes us for working efficiently. The faster we get, the less we make. Nobody
on either side of that contract is rooting for speed.

**Fixed bids.** Fixed bids force us to pad. The bigger the unknowns, the
bigger the pad. The buyer pays for risk that may never materialize, and any
mid-project change request becomes a fight about scope.

## What we landed on

We sell flat-rate blocks of capacity. A block buys a known number of hours at a
known price. You decide when to spend them. We commit to running our four-day
production cycle against whatever is queued up that week.

The contract is simple:

- **You** keep your block topped up and respond to update emails on Day 2 and
  Day 4 so the cycle does not stall.
- **We** show up every cycle and ship every cycle.

Unused time does not expire. If you need to pause for a quarter, your blocks
are waiting when you come back. If we built something that does not need more
work for a while, you are not paying retainer for silence.

## Why it works

Blocks turn the conversation from "what does this cost?" into "what is this
worth?" Once a client sees what one block produces, the next purchase decision
is mechanical — they know the unit economics. We do not have to negotiate scope
every time the goal moves an inch.

It is also the only model we have found that survives clients who genuinely
want to ship fast. When the buyer's incentive and the builder's incentive
finally point in the same direction, the project moves.
